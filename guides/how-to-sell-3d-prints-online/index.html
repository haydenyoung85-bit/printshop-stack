How to sell 3D prints online

Where to sell 3D prints online, Etsy's fees and original-design rules, running your own Shopify store, SEO tools and model licensing.

Selling 3D prints online comes down to three decisions: what you are allowed to sell, where you sell it, and how buyers will find it. This guide covers all three, using the marketplaces' own policy pages.

Start with the rights to what you print

Printing a file does not give you the right to sell it. Many free models are shared under licenses such as Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial, which allows sharing and adapting but not commercial use (source 7). Read the license on every file you plan to sell. If it is unclear, ask the designer or design your own.

Etsy has its own rules on top of licensing. Items made with computerized tools such as 3D printers fall under "Made by a seller," and listings must use your own photos (source 1). Etsy's Creativity Standards cover what qualifies as your original design (source 2). Read the current version before you list printed items you did not design. If someone else prints your designs for you, Etsy requires you to disclose that production partner on your listings (source 3).

Using AI-generated models? Check the generator's license terms before selling, and note that Etsy requires seller-prompted AI creations to disclose the use of AI (source 1).

Choose a channel

Marketplace: Etsy

A marketplace brings buyers who are already searching. In exchange you pay per listing and per sale. Etsy charges $0.20 per listing and a 6.5% transaction fee on the item price plus any shipping you charge (source 4). Payment processing and, on sales from Offsite Ads, an advertising fee come on top (source 5). The pricing guide shows how to build these into your price.

Your own store: Shopify

Your own store gives you your brand, your domain and your customer relationships, but you have to bring the traffic yourself. Shopify charges a monthly plan fee plus payment processing. Our listing has the plan prices we found on its pricing page (source 6). Many shops start on a marketplace and add their own store once repeat buyers show up.

Good photos and clear descriptions matter as much as keywords. Show scale, color accuracy and surface finish. State the material, size and lead time, and say whether items are printed to order.

Before you take orders

  • Know your capacity. Print-farm tools can help you queue orders across printers. See Best software to manage a 3D print farm.
  • Set realistic processing times that include failed prints and post-processing.
  • Keep materials consistent, so a reorder looks like the first order. See filament brands.
  • Recheck the fee and policy pages above regularly. Marketplaces change them.
